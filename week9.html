Mini Peer Review Adventure

Adapted from Jennifer Geraci. Below are some helpful and optional peer review prompts. Choose whichever works for you, and mix and match questions if you want to.

1

Main Argument Check

Have your peer read through your entire essay and type out what they think your main argument is: make a point, change perspective, attack, or defend.

Is it a personal argument, like the best food for you; a universal argument, like the best food for everyone; a culturally specific argument, like the best food for a particular culture; or something else?

You do the same for your peer's paper. After you are both finished, share what you think the main message is with your peer. Work together to revise your drafts so the main message is clearer for readers.

2

Introduction Help

If you need help with your introduction, share introduction sections with each other. Both partners write margin comments about their reactions and write out questions where parts are unclear.

Then, have a conversation about the comments and help each other make revisions.

3

New Stuff

Share a new or recently written part of your draft with your peer, and have your peer share theirs with you. Talk about what you added, why you added that part, and what effect you hope the new stuff will have on readers.

Help each other clarify, add to, or change the new stuff as needed.

4

Do Your Own Peer Review

You and your peer can create your own peer review based on your draft's revision needs. Be rebels.

5

Work Alone

FINE. Work alone. I see how you are.

Revision Focus

This activity turns peer review into a choice-based workshop: writers can focus on the main argument, the introduction, a new section, or whatever revision question their draft most needs.
