Category 1

Reading and Annotating

Tasker Annotation

Finding tone inside word choice

My comments on Tasker's Rattlesnake Burrito review show an early shift from noticing content to noticing technique. I focused on phrases such as "slim and pale," "mournful pillow-soft sleeve," and "methodically distributed" because they made the food feel dramatic, strange, and almost unsafe.

This helped me understand that analysis is not only about explaining what a text means. It is also about showing how a writer's choices guide the reader's emotional response.

Wendig Annotation

Voice as argument

Wendig's apple ranking taught me to read for persona. His humor, exaggeration, and mock-serious ranking style showed me that a writer can turn an ordinary topic into a performance of taste, judgment, and cultural commentary.

After this annotation, I became more aware of voice in my own writing: not just whether my sentences were correct, but what kind of speaker they created.

Category 2

Process, Drafting, Workshopping, and Revision

Food Fight Revision

Paragraphs with a job

The body paragraph workshop made revision more concrete for me. I learned to identify whether a paragraph was only discussing a topic or actually advancing a claim. My revision plan became: narrow the paragraph's purpose, add precise evidence, and slow down the analysis.

Open paragraph workshop

Conclusion Workshop

Ending with consequence

In the conclusion workshop, I practiced moving beyond summary. A strong ending should return to the essay's central conflict and show what the reader now understands differently about food, memory, belonging, and cultural value.

Category 3

The Sides

Description for Effect

Practicing control before the larger assignments

The Honey Chicken Super Bowl side helped me practice low-stakes stylistic control. I tried to influence the reader's mood through pacing, repetition, contrast, and abstract language. The peer comment also gave me a revision direction: use more emphasis and metaphor to make the imitation stronger.

This side mattered because it let me test techniques before using them in bigger assignments. It made writing feel less like producing one perfect draft and more like trying a method, seeing what works, and adjusting.

Realizing description is never neutral

In my first cookbook reflection, I wrote that language can shape the way we experience ordinary food. This became one of the main ideas of my portfolio: word choice, sentence order, and tone do not decorate meaning; they create it.
