BF Conclusions Workshop

WR40 · Revision Workshop Ann Ge · May 18

Conclusion Revision Notes

In this workshop, I focused on making the Food Fight conclusion do more than repeat the introduction. The strongest ending should return to the essay's central conflict, show what the reader now understands differently, and leave the food debate with larger cultural stakes.

My revision goal is to move from summary into consequence. Instead of ending with a general statement about food and identity, I want the conclusion to explain why the argument matters after the reader has seen the evidence: food arguments are also arguments about memory, belonging, value, and who gets to define a tradition.

The workshop also reminded me that a conclusion can echo an image from the beginning of the essay. Returning to a concrete food detail can make the final paragraph feel connected to the whole draft while still moving the argument forward.

Revision Focus

A stronger conclusion should answer the quiet reader question: now that I have followed this argument, what should I notice, question, or value differently?

Draft Two asks me to treat revision as rethinking, not only correcting. Before submitting the second draft, I need to check whether the thesis is specific, whether each paragraph advances the same argument, and whether the evidence is explained instead of simply inserted.

Paragraph Purpose

Workshop: Best Body Paragraph, Worst Body Paragraph

WR40 · Workshop Ann Ge · May 20

Best Body Paragraph

My strongest body paragraph is the one where the topic sentence makes a claim instead of announcing a topic. It connects a specific food example to the essay's larger question about culture and judgment, so the paragraph has a reason to exist inside the argument.

This paragraph works because the evidence is followed by explanation. I do not want the reader to assume the connection; I want the paragraph to show how the detail changes the meaning of the food debate.

Why It Works

The paragraph has a clear topic sentence, concrete evidence, analysis after the evidence, and a final sentence that points back to the thesis.

WR40 · Workshop Ann Ge · May 20

Worst Body Paragraph

My weakest body paragraph is weaker because it explains an idea in broad terms but does not use enough specific evidence. The paragraph sounds connected to the essay, but the reader may not be able to see exactly how the example proves the point.

To revise it, I need to narrow the paragraph's purpose, add a more precise example, and slow down the analysis. The paragraph should not only say that food has cultural meaning; it should show which meaning is being debated and why that debate matters.

What Needs Work

The main problem is not grammar. The main problem is that the paragraph needs sharper evidence, clearer stakes, and a stronger bridge back to the essay's claim.

1

Rewrite topic sentences

Each topic sentence should make a small claim that supports the larger thesis.

2

Add evidence before explaining

Use a concrete moment, quote, or food detail so the analysis has something specific to interpret.

3

End with consequence

Close each paragraph by clarifying how the point changes the reader's understanding of the debate.

Final Portfolio Prompt

WR40 · Portfolio Planning Ann Ge · May 20

Final Portfolio Prompt Notes

The final portfolio asks me to collect work from across the quarter and present it as evidence of growth. The portfolio should not feel like a storage folder. It should guide a reader through my writing process, showing how annotation, imitation, workshop, drafting, and revision are connected.

For my reflective introduction, I can explain how my writing changed from describing food to making arguments about food. Early assignments helped me notice sensory detail and style; later assignments pushed me to organize evidence, respond to feedback, and make claims with clearer stakes.

This prompt also helps me think about design. The archive needs to be easy to navigate, each week should have a clear purpose, and the final reflection should explain why these pieces belong together as one portfolio.

Portfolio Strategy

I will use the weekly pages to show process, then use the final reflection to connect those pieces into a story about growth, revision, and stronger argument.

WR40 · Cookbook Reflection Ann Ge · Week 08

The Cookbook: Brainstorm!

This cookbook entry helped me begin thinking about the final portfolio as a complete project instead of a set of separate assignments. I brainstormed which pieces best show my growth, which drafts need the most revision, and how the weekly archive can tell a clear story about my writing process.

One important idea from this brainstorm is that my portfolio should show movement. Early work can show how I practiced description, annotation, and imitation, while the Food Fight unit can show how I learned to build a more focused argument with evidence, stakes, and revision.

For the reflective introduction, I want to connect these pieces through a few major themes: learning to make food writing more specific, learning to use sources more purposefully, and learning that revision is a way of changing the essay's thinking, not just fixing sentences.

Portfolio Brainstorm

The cookbook gives me a place to plan the final portfolio's organization, choose evidence of growth, and prepare the reflective introduction.
